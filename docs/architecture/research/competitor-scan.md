## 3. Synthesis

### Common tier structures
1. Three or four named paid tiers plus Free/Starter and an "Enterprise - contact sales" level (Eat App: Free/Lite/Core/Pro/Enterprise; SevenRooms: Starter/Growth/Premium; OpenTable: Basic/Core/Pro per S).
2. Subscription per venue/branch; multi-venue groups pushed to quote-based Enterprise.
3. A la carte add-ons priced separately (Eat App waitlist, deposits, messaging, POS/payments at 19-49/mo each; SevenRooms email, WhatsApp, API, voice AI).
4. Discovery networks charge per diner (OpenTable; SevenRooms marketplaces), while software-only vendors (Resy, Eat App) advertise no per-cover fee.
5. India: discovery apps are commission-based and unpublished; POS vendors (Petpooja) sell module bundles in INR with reservations as a top-bundle module.

### What is typically gated by tier
- POS and payment integrations (Eat App Pro or +39; OpenTable Pro; Resy Premium; Square Plus for OpenTable sync).
- Waitlist, deposits/prepayment/card capture, event ticketing (add-on or mid/high tier).
- Messaging volume (WhatsApp allowances), email marketing, automation.
- Analytics and reporting; CRM depth.
- API, PMS integration, multi-venue/group tooling, dedicated success manager (top tier or Enterprise).
- Petpooja's Table Reservation Manager sits in the top Operations bundle only.

### Five feature gaps / opportunities seen in reviews
Review base is thin (Capterra Eat App about 9 reviews; AU-specific reviews sparse). Directional only.
1. Cost and per-cover fees on discovery networks: OpenTable's top cons on Capterra/G2 are high cost and limited customisation.
2. Limited customisation (SevenRooms and OpenTable reviewers on G2).
3. Capacity rules by table count rather than covers: an Eat App reviewer asked for table-count limits instead of cover-based restrictions.
4. Support responsiveness at smaller vendors: an Eat App reviewer called support "almost non existent"; SevenRooms vs Eat App comparisons split support vs roadmap.
5. Guest messaging control and cost: OpenTable reviewer complaint about repetitive reminder texts; WhatsApp caps gate lower Eat App tiers. Related structural gap: transparent multi-branch group pricing is largely absent (groups go to quote-based Enterprise), and the closures of Dimmi/TheFork and Quandoo in AU leave less local competition among booking networks.

## 4. Pricing-model inputs checklist (no recommended prices)

- [ ] Fully loaded cost to serve per branch per month (hosting, support, success staff, third-party API fees).
- [ ] Notification costs per branch (WhatsApp/SMS/email per message, AUD and INR) vs included allowances.
- [ ] Sales commission: default 30%; confirm basis (first-year vs recurring, per-branch vs per-contract) and its effect on net revenue per branch.
- [ ] Billing-service fees (percentage plus fixed, per invoice, failed payments, FX) and tax treatment (AU GST 10%, India GST 18%).
- [ ] Payment-processor fees if deposits/prepayments are offered; whether Cheery takes a prepayment percentage (Tock/Resy observed 2-3%).
- [ ] Churn, annual-prepay discount (competitors show up to 30% yearly discount) and trial assumptions.
- [ ] Multi-branch group discounting and the year-1 target (10 customers / 100 branches, about 10 branches per customer).
- [ ] Tier boundaries: which modules (waitlist, CRM, deposits, integrations, API, analytics) are gated; add-on pricing.
- [ ] Per-diner/per-cover fee decision (Resy and Eat App advertise none; OpenTable and SevenRooms marketplaces charge).
- [ ] Contract term and cancellation policy (OpenTable observed: 1 year auto-renew, 30 days' notice; Square: no lock-in).
- [ ] Separate AUD and INR price points (local market, not currency conversion of competitor prices).
- [ ] Break-even and payback per branch after CAC, commission and billing fees.
